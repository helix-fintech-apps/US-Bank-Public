Family finance

How to set an allowance your kids will actually learn from

Pick an amount that matches what you expect them to pay for themselves, not just a number that sounds right. If the allowance covers snacks and games, they learn to plan; if it covers nothing, it's just a gift.

Pay on the same day every week, and split it on arrival: some to spend, some to save toward a goal they chose. Let them make small mistakes now, when the stakes are a few dollars.

Tie extra money to chores you approve, and keep the base allowance separate, so they learn both that money is earned and that budgeting matters.

Credit

What really moves your credit score (and what doesn't)

Paying on time matters most: a single payment 30 or more days late can stay on your report for years. Next is how much of your available credit you use. Try to keep each card's balance well under its limit.

Checking your own score is a soft inquiry and never lowers it. Applying for new credit is a hard inquiry, which can lower it a little for a while.

Your income, your bank balance and where you shop don't appear on a credit report at all.

Security

5 signs a payment request is a scam

  1. It's urgent: "pay now or your account closes".
  2. They ask for a one-time code. USA Banc will never ask you for one.
  3. They want gift cards, crypto or a wire to someone you've never met.
  4. The sender's email or number is slightly off.
  5. They "accidentally" sent you money and want it back another way.

When in doubt, hang up and call the number on the back of your card. Report anything suspicious on our security page.

Saving

Saving for college: start small, start early

Even $50 a month from birth adds up to more than $14,000 by age 18 before any earnings, and much more with interest. Automatic transfers make it painless.

A custodial account (UTMA) holds savings in your child's name. A 529 plan offers tax advantages for education. Compare both before you choose.
